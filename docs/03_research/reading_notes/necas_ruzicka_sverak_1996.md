# Reading notes: Necas-Ruzicka-Sverak (1996), with Tsai (1998)

J. Necas, M. Ruzicka, V. Sverak, "On Leray's self-similar solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations," Acta Mathematica 176, no. 2 (1996), 283-294.

T.-P. Tsai, "On Leray's self-similar solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations satisfying local energy estimates," Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis 143, no. 1 (1998), 29-51.

> Leray (1934) proposed backward self-similar solutions as the most natural candidate finite-time singularity for 3D Navier-Stokes: a single profile $U$ that, replayed at finer and finer scales as $t \to T$, would concentrate to a point. Necas-Ruzicka-Sverak prove this candidate does not exist: any such profile $U$ that lies in $L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ must be identically zero, so the solution it generates is the trivial one. Tsai removes the $L^3$ hypothesis, replacing it with a local energy bound that any physically reasonable singular solution would satisfy, and reaches the same conclusion. This is Architecture 4 (blow-up and self-similar solutions) acting as an exclusion: it kills the simplest blow-up mechanism. The proof is a Liouville-type rigidity argument that uses the viscous term essentially (control C), so it is exactly the kind of result that distinguishes Navier-Stokes from inviscid Burgers and Euler, both of which do form singularities. It does not use 3D vortex stretching, and it does not touch the supercriticality gap (control B), which is the honest limit of what it gives.

## The Leray ansatz

Leray's backward self-similar ansatz writes a velocity field that is invariant under the Navier-Stokes scaling symmetry, anchored at a putative blow-up time $T$. With $a > 0$ a fixed constant the ansatz is

$$u(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2a(T-t)}}\; U\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2a(T-t)}}\right), \qquad t < T,$$

$$p(x,t) = \frac{1}{2a(T-t)}\; P\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2a(T-t)}}\right),$$

where $y = x/\sqrt{2a(T-t)}$ is the self-similar variable and $(U, P)$ are the time-independent profile fields. This is precisely the orbit of the scaling $u_\lambda(x,t) = \lambda\, u(\lambda x, \lambda^2 t)$ with $\lambda = 1/\sqrt{2a(T-t)}$: the field reproduces itself at scale $\lambda(t) \to \infty$ as $t \to T^-$, so a nontrivial $U$ would describe energy collapsing to the origin at the singular time. Substituting the ansatz into the Navier-Stokes momentum equation $\partial_t u + (u\cdot\nabla) u = -\nabla p + \Delta u$ (unit viscosity) and using $\partial_t \lambda(t)$ converts the PDE into a stationary nonlinear elliptic system for $U$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, the **Leray profile equation**:

$$-\Delta U + a\,(U + (y\cdot\nabla) U) + (U\cdot\nabla) U + \nabla P = 0, \qquad \nabla\cdot U = 0.$$

The term $a\,(U + (y\cdot\nabla)U)$ is the linear "self-similar drift" generated by the time differentiation. It is the structural fingerprint of the backward ansatz: it carries a definite sign that the proof exploits. The whole question of Leray self-similar blow-up reduces to: does this elliptic system have a nontrivial solution $U$ with appropriate decay?

## What it proves

### Necas-Ruzicka-Sverak (1996), the $L^3$ exclusion

**Theorem (NRS).** Let $U \in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^3)$ be a weak solution of the Leray profile equation above with $a > 0$, and suppose additionally that

$$U \in L^3(\mathbb{R}^3).$$

Then $U \equiv 0$.

Consequently the only backward self-similar solution of Leray's form with the corresponding finite-energy / critical decay is the trivial one, and there is no finite-time singularity of Leray self-similar type in this class. The hypothesis $U \in L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is the natural one: under the scaling, $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)} = \|U\|_{L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)}$ is constant in $t$ (see Criticality placement), so $L^3$ membership of the profile is exactly the statement that the generated solution has bounded critical Lebesgue norm up to the singular time. A blow-up that respected the ESS endpoint barrier (control of $L^\infty_t L^3_x$, Escauriaza-Seregin-Sverak 2003) at the level of the profile would have to live here, and NRS shows it cannot.

### Tsai (1998), the local-energy extension

Tsai's contribution is to weaken the global $L^3$ hypothesis to a **local energy estimate** of the type that suitable weak solutions (in the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg sense) automatically satisfy, and to push the rigidity through under those weaker assumptions.

**Theorem (Tsai).** A Leray backward self-similar solution whose profile $U$ satisfies a local energy bound (the generalized energy inequality for suitable weak solutions, equivalently a local Morrey/energy control on $U$ rather than global $L^3$ integrability) is trivial: $U \equiv 0$. Tsai further analyzes the associated profile pressure and shows that, under these assumptions, the candidate singular solution must be smooth, and in fact zero.

Two structural points sharpen the picture:

- Tsai shows the exclusion is not an artifact of demanding global integrability. The obstruction is local-to-mesoscale rigidity, so it survives in the realistic class of suitable weak solutions, where only local energy is controlled. This is the version that matters for the Clay problem, because Leray-Hopf / CKN solutions carry local energy estimates but are not known to lie in $L^\infty_t L^3_x$.
- Tsai also addresses **forward** self-similar solutions (the $t > 0$, expanding analog), where the linear drift carries the opposite sign and the rigidity does **not** hold. Forward self-similar solutions can be nontrivial; they are not singularities (they describe self-similar spreading from singular initial data, not collapse), and their existence was later established constructively by Jia-Sverak (2014). The asymmetry between the backward (excluded) and forward (existent) cases is the sign of $a\,(U + (y\cdot\nabla)U)$, and it is the heart of why the argument is a genuine viscous rigidity rather than a soft scaling statement.

## Method / structure

The mechanism in NRS is a **Liouville-type / maximum-principle rigidity** for the head pressure, built on the viscous elliptic structure of the profile equation. At the lemma level the chain is:

1. **Reduction to a stationary elliptic system.** As above, the backward ansatz turns the parabolic Navier-Stokes system into the stationary Leray profile equation on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the extra linear drift $a\,(U + (y\cdot\nabla)U)$. The viscous Laplacian $-\Delta U$ survives the reduction. This is essential: drop it and the elliptic system loses its smoothing and its maximum principle, and the argument collapses. The viscosity is load-bearing, not decorative.

2. **The head pressure / Bernoulli-type quantity.** Form the scalar "head pressure" $\Pi = P + \tfrac12 |U|^2$ (the profile analog of the Bernoulli function). A computation using the equation and $\nabla\cdot U = 0$ shows $\Pi$ satisfies a drift-diffusion inequality of the form

$$-\Delta \Pi + b\cdot \nabla \Pi \le 0 \quad\text{(in the relevant region),}$$

a differential inequality with the right sign for a **maximum principle**. The self-similar drift term contributes the favorable sign; this is exactly where the backward (collapsing) case differs from the forward (expanding) case.

3. **Decay from integrability, then the maximum principle bites.** The integrability hypothesis ($U \in L^3$ in NRS; local energy plus the profile pressure analysis in Tsai) forces $U$ and $\Pi$ to decay at spatial infinity in the self-similar variable $y$. A subsolution of $-\Delta \Pi + b\cdot\nabla\Pi \le 0$ that decays at infinity attains no interior positive maximum, so $\Pi$ cannot have a strict interior maximum; chasing the sign through the inequality forces $\Pi$, and then $U$, to be constant, and the decay pins the constant to zero. Hence $U \equiv 0$.

4. **Tsai's localization.** Tsai replaces the global $L^3$ decay input by local energy estimates, controls the profile pressure $P$ via the elliptic pressure equation $-\Delta P = \partial_i \partial_j (U_i U_j)$ in the self-similar frame, and runs the same head-pressure maximum-principle argument with the weaker decay. The technical work is in extracting enough decay of $\Pi$ from local energy alone; the rigidity engine is the same.

The single most important structural observation: **the proof never touches vortex stretching $\omega\cdot\nabla u$**. It is a statement about the stationary profile elliptic system, and it works by a scalar maximum principle on a Bernoulli-type quantity. The 3D-ness enters only through the dimension-dependent constants and the decay exponents, not through the stretching mechanism that the project identifies as the true engine of potential 3D blow-up.

## Criticality placement

The Leray ansatz is built on the scaling symmetry itself, so every quantity sits at a sharply defined place on the sub/critical/super scale of `experiments/_shared/criticality.py`.

- **The profile lives at criticality.** Under $u_\lambda(x,t) = \lambda\, u(\lambda x, \lambda^2 t)$ the spatial $L^3$ norm has scaling exponent $a = 1 - d/q = 1 - 3/3 = 0$ (`lebesgue_exponent(3, 3)` returns $0$): $L^3$ is exactly critical. The self-similar solution is the orbit on which the critical norm is **constant in time**, $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^3} = \|U\|_{L^3}$. So the NRS hypothesis $U \in L^3$ is the statement "the candidate singularity sits exactly at the critical level," and NRS says nothing nontrivial lives there of Leray's form. This is the same critical level as the ESS endpoint (`L^3 (Escauriaza-Seregin-Sverak endpoint)` in `standard_norms_3d()`), and it is no accident: both are statements about the borderline critical Lebesgue norm.

- **Tsai's local-energy class is critical too.** The local energy quantity $\sup_{x_0,r}\, r^{-1}\!\int_{B_r(x_0)} |u|^2$ (the $L^2$-based scale-invariant Morrey quantity) is scale invariant in 3D, hence critical. Tsai is the version of the exclusion phrased at this critical local level rather than the global $L^3$ level.

- **What the exclusion does NOT reach: the supercritical energy.** The criticality bookkeeper flags the only all-time a priori bound, the energy norm $\|u\|_{L^2}$, as supercritical: `lebesgue_exponent(2, 3)` $= 1 - 3/2 = -1/2 < 0$, so `audit_estimate` returns `INSUFFICIENT_BY_ITSELF`. NRS/Tsai do not change this. They rule out one shape of singularity at the critical level; they do not supply a coercive critical a priori bound. The supercriticality gap (control B) is untouched, and that is the structural reason the result is an exclusion, not a regularity theorem. It removes a branch of the search tree; it does not provide the missing critical control.

In bookkeeper terms: NRS/Tsai is a `AT_THE_MARGIN` statement (it concerns a critical norm) that establishes a **negative** fact (no nontrivial Leray profile at this margin), rather than the positive a priori bound the margin would need.

## Against the three controls

- **(A) 2D control.** The argument does not use 3D structure and would, in fact, transfer to 2D: the head-pressure maximum principle is dimension-robust, and in 2D it likewise excludes nontrivial backward Leray self-similar profiles. But this is **consistent**, not a failure of control A, precisely because the result is an exclusion. Control A forbids a method that would predict 2D **blow-up**; NRS/Tsai predicts no blow-up of this type in either dimension, which is the correct verdict in 2D (where there is genuinely no blow-up). The honest reading: NRS/Tsai is not a regularity mechanism that needs to distinguish 2D from 3D, so control A does not bind it. It also means the result cannot be the seed of a 3D regularity proof, because it never engages the stretching term $\omega\cdot\nabla u$ that separates the two dimensions.

- **(B) Supercriticality.** The result lives at the critical level ($L^3$, scale-invariant local energy) and proves a negative there. It does not produce a coercive global-in-time bound at the critical or subcritical level, so it does not close the supercriticality gap and does not claim to. Energy-level estimates are neither used nor improved. Verdict: passes the discipline (it does not pretend energy suffices), but it is not regularity-closing, and it is not meant to be.

- **(C) Viscosity / Burgers.** This is the control NRS/Tsai engages essentially and the reason it belongs in this dossier. The viscous Laplacian $-\Delta U$ is exactly what supplies the maximum principle for the head pressure $\Pi$. Strip the viscosity and you are looking at the Euler self-similar profile equation, which has **no** such smoothing and where the rigidity fails: inviscid models (Burgers shocks, and Euler with its modern $C^{1,\alpha}$ finite-time singularity, Elgindi 2021) do produce self-similar-type singularities. NRS/Tsai is a clean demonstration that the viscous term is doing real work to forbid the simplest collapse. A method blind to viscosity could not produce this exclusion, which is precisely what control C demands a good NS argument respect.

## What it gives / what it does not give

**What it gives.**

- It removes Leray's own proposed singularity mechanism, the most natural and longest-standing candidate, from the table in the critical class. After NRS/Tsai, any Navier-Stokes finite-time singularity is **not** exactly backward self-similar of Leray's form with critical/local-energy decay. This is a genuine coordinate: the real singularity, if it exists, must be a strictly more complicated object.
- It cleanly demonstrates the viscous rigidity (control C): the proof's dependence on $-\Delta$ shows where Navier-Stokes structurally departs from Euler in the self-similar problem.
- It fixes the boundary between excluded (backward, collapsing) and possible (forward, expanding) self-similar objects, via the sign of the self-similar drift, which orients all later work on self-similar Navier-Stokes.

**What it does not give (the gap to closing regularity).**

- **It is one shape, not all shapes.** The exclusion is specific to exact backward self-similarity. It leaves open:
  - **Discretely self-similar (DSS) blow-up**: invariance only under a discrete subgroup of the scaling, $u(x,t) = \lambda\, u(\lambda x, \lambda^2 t)$ for one fixed $\lambda > 1$ rather than all $\lambda$. The maximum-principle rigidity does not apply, and DSS singularities are not excluded.
  - **Asymptotically self-similar blow-up**: a singularity that only resembles a self-similar profile in a rescaling limit, without being exactly self-similar at finite times. This is the generic expectation if blow-up occurs, and it is not touched.
  - **Type II (non-self-similar-rate) blow-up**: faster-than-self-similar concentration. Outside the ansatz entirely.
- **It supplies no a priori bound.** The supercriticality gap (control B) is exactly where it does not help. Closing regularity needs a coercive critical-or-subcritical global bound; NRS/Tsai provides none. It narrows the search by eliminating a candidate; it does not advance the front by adding control.
- **It does not engage vortex stretching.** Because the proof is a scalar maximum principle on the profile, it says nothing about the 3D mechanism (stretching $\omega\cdot\nabla u$) that the project identifies as the actual engine of any genuine 3D blow-up. The structural lesson is almost the opposite: the simplest scenario is killed by viscosity without any reference to stretching, so the dangerous scenarios are precisely the ones where stretching outruns the viscous smoothing on which this proof relies.

The honest one-line summary for the spine: NRS/Tsai converts "the natural singularity" into "a dead branch," using viscosity essentially, while leaving the supercriticality gap and the stretching question wide open.

## Lineage and sharpest known form

**Builds on.**

- J. Leray, Acta Math. 63 (1934): proposed the backward self-similar ansatz as a candidate singularity. NRS/Tsai is the resolution, sixty-odd years later, that the candidate is empty in the critical class. See [`leray_1934.md`](leray_1934.md).
- The ESS critical-norm philosophy: $L^3$ is the borderline space, and the self-similar profile question is naturally posed there. See [`escauriaza_seregin_sverak_2003.md`](escauriaza_seregin_sverak_2003.md). NRS predates ESS but sits at the same critical level, and the ESS blow-up rescaling (extracting an ancient bounded solution and killing it by backward uniqueness) is the descendant rigidity that does engage a genuine NS mechanism beyond the maximum principle.

**Built on it / refinements through 2025.**

- **Forward self-similar existence.** H. Jia, V. Sverak, "Local-in-space estimates near initial time for weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations and forward self-similar solutions," Invent. Math. 196 (2014), 233-265: constructs nontrivial **forward** self-similar solutions for $(-1)$-homogeneous initial data, smooth for $t > 0$, via a local-in-space short-time smoothing estimate. This is the positive counterpart to the NRS/Tsai negative: the backward case is rigid (empty), the forward case is rich (existent). The two together complete Leray's program.
- **The non-uniqueness conjecture.** H. Jia, V. Sverak, "Are the incompressible 3d Navier-Stokes equations locally ill-posed in the natural energy space?," J. Funct. Anal. 268 (2015), 3734-3766: gives a spectral sufficient condition (an unstable eigenvalue of a linearization around a forward self-similar profile) under which **two distinct** scale-invariant solutions emanate from the same $(-1)$-homogeneous data, hence non-uniqueness and ill-posedness for benign compactly-supported-type data at the borderline of perturbation theory. Guillod-Sverak (2017, numerics) gave strong evidence the spectral condition holds. This is the seed of the modern non-uniqueness program. It is an Architecture-5-adjacent statement: it is about non-uniqueness, not about smooth-flow regularity, so it sits partly outside the three-control discipline (see [`05_convex_integration_boundary.md`](../research_directions/05_convex_integration_boundary.md)).
- **Forced Leray-Hopf non-uniqueness.** D. Albritton, E. Brue, M. Colombo, "Non-uniqueness of Leray solutions of the forced Navier-Stokes equations," Ann. of Math. 196 (2022): rigorously realizes a Jia-Sverak-type instability with a force, producing two Leray-Hopf solutions from zero data. Built conceptually on the self-similar instability picture that NRS/Tsai/Jia-Sverak set up.
- **Profiles in larger spaces.** Subsequent work pushes the NRS exclusion and the forward existence into Morrey and Marcinkiewicz spaces (e.g. Guevara-Phuc; Lai-Miao-Zheng; Bradshaw-Tsai on DSS), and Tsai's "Self-similar solutions to the nonstationary Navier-Stokes equations" survey (in Handbook of Mathematical Analysis in Mechanics of Viscous Fluids) is the current map. The sharpest framing as of 2025: backward exact self-similar collapse is excluded across the natural critical/local-energy and Morrey classes; the open frontier is DSS and asymptotically self-similar collapse, both unresolved.

**Sharpest known form (2025).** Exact backward Leray self-similar singularities are excluded for profiles in $L^3$ (NRS), for profiles satisfying local energy estimates / suitable-weak-solution decay (Tsai), and in the natural Morrey-type generalizations. Forward self-similar solutions exist (Jia-Sverak 2014). Non-uniqueness from a forward self-similar instability is established for forced Leray-Hopf solutions (Albritton-Brue-Colombo 2022) and conjectured (with numerical support) for the unforced critical-data problem (Jia-Sverak 2015, Guillod-Sverak). Discretely self-similar and asymptotically self-similar **backward** (singular) scenarios remain open.
