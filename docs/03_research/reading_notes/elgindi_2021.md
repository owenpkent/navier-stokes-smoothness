# Reading notes: Elgindi (2021), finite-time singularity for $C^{1,\alpha}$ Euler

Tarek M. Elgindi, "Finite-time singularity formation for $C^{1,\alpha}$ solutions to the incompressible Euler equations on $\mathbb{R}^3$," Annals of Mathematics (2) 194, no. 3 (2021), 647-727.

Companion (stability and the 2D Boussinesq analogue): T. M. Elgindi, T. Ghoul, N. Masmoudi, "On the stability of self-similar blow-up for $C^{1,\alpha}$ solutions to the incompressible Euler equations, and globally regular Boussinesq solutions," / published as "On Stability and Instability of $C^{1,\alpha}$ Singular Solutions to the 3D Euler and 2D Boussinesq Equations," Communications in Mathematical Physics 405 (2024), article 90. (arXiv:1910.14071, 2019.) (verify: exact CMP article number/year against published version.)

> This is the first rigorous proof that a $C^{1,\alpha}$ (Holder-continuous velocity gradient) finite-energy solution of the 3D incompressible **Euler** equations forms a singularity in finite time. It is the cleanest possible statement of control C: with viscosity switched off, 3D incompressible flow genuinely blows up, and it does so by exactly the vortex-stretching mechanism ($\omega\cdot\nabla u$) that the project flags as the engine of any potential 3D singularity. The construction is axisymmetric **without swirl**, lives at low regularity (only $C^{1,\alpha}$, not $C^\infty$), and is driven by a stable self-similar profile of a fundamental/leading-order one-dimensional model. It is Architecture 4 (blow-up and self-similar solutions), and it is the inviscid extremal that isolates precisely what the Navier-Stokes dissipation must beat: a self-similar collapse at the borderline of the classical local well-posedness theory. It says nothing about Navier-Stokes regularity directly. Its job in the spine is to fix the target. Viscosity is not decoration; it is the only thing standing between this scenario and a Navier-Stokes singularity, so a regularity proof must use dissipation against this exact structure.

## What it proves

### Statement (Elgindi 2021, main theorem)

**Theorem (Elgindi).** There exists $\alpha_0 > 0$ such that for every $0 < \alpha < \alpha_0$ the following holds. There is a divergence-free initial velocity field $u_0$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ that is

- axisymmetric with **no swirl** (in cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ the velocity has $u^\theta \equiv 0$, so only the meridional components are nonzero),
- $C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^3)$ (continuous velocity gradient, Holder continuous with exponent $\alpha$),
- compactly supported / finite energy ($u_0 \in L^2$), and odd in $z$,

such that the unique local-in-time solution $u(\cdot, t)$ of the 3D incompressible Euler equations
$$\partial_t u + (u\cdot\nabla) u = -\nabla p, \qquad \nabla\cdot u = 0, \qquad u(\cdot, 0) = u_0,$$
with this data remains in $C^{1,\alpha}$ on a maximal interval $[0, T^*)$ with $T^* < \infty$ and **cannot be continued** past $T^*$ as a $C^{1,\alpha}$ (equivalently, as a $C^1$ with the corresponding norm bound) solution. The blow-up is quantified by
$$\int_0^{T^*} \|\omega(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty}\, dt = +\infty,$$
the Beale-Kato-Majda quantity, with $\|\omega(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty} \to \infty$ as $t \to T^*$. The singularity is at the spatial origin and the blow-up is (asymptotically) self-similar: in suitable rescaled variables the solution converges to a fixed self-similar profile.

Two structural features of the hypotheses carry the whole result.

- **No swirl.** For axisymmetric flow without swirl the only vorticity component is the azimuthal one, $\omega = \omega^\theta\, e_\theta$, and the key transported quantity is $\omega^\theta / r$. In the **smooth** ($C^\infty$, indeed Lipschitz-velocity) class this is exactly the setting in which classical results forbid certain singularities (the no-swirl axisymmetric Euler vorticity has a transport structure reminiscent of 2D), and global regularity is known for smooth swirl-free axisymmetric flow under additional decay. Elgindi shows that dropping the regularity from Lipschitz/$C^1$-smooth down to $C^{1,\alpha}$ is enough to **break** that protection. The singularity lives precisely in the gap between $C^{1,\alpha}$ and $C^\infty$.

- **Low regularity is the mechanism, not a technicality.** At $C^{1,\alpha}$ the velocity gradient is finite but the second derivatives need not be bounded, so the Biot-Savart law produces a velocity gradient that is, at leading order, governed by a singular-integral (logarithmically divergent in the smooth limit) operator that the $C^{1,\alpha}$ scaling **regularizes into a bounded but order-one amplification**. This order-one feedback of vorticity onto its own stretching rate is the engine. In the $\alpha \to 0$ limit the amplification is exactly the borderline log-divergence that the classical $C^1$ theory (and BKM) sit on; for $\alpha > 0$ small it is a genuine, controllable, blow-up-producing term.

### Companion statement (Elgindi-Ghoul-Masmoudi): stability and the 2D Boussinesq analogue

The companion paper proves that the self-similar blow-up of the main theorem is **stable**: it persists under a (suitably $C^{1,\alpha}$-class) open set of perturbations of the initial data, including perturbations that switch on a small swirl, so the singularity is not a fragile codimension-high object. The same analysis transfers to the **2D Boussinesq** system
$$\partial_t \omega + (u\cdot\nabla)\omega = \partial_{x_1}\rho, \qquad \partial_t \rho + (u\cdot\nabla)\rho = 0, \qquad u = \nabla^\perp\Delta^{-1}\omega,$$
on the half-plane, which is the standard low-dimensional model for axisymmetric Euler near the symmetry axis (the density gradient $\partial_{x_1}\rho$ plays the role of the vortex-stretching forcing). The companion establishes a corresponding stable $C^{1,\alpha}$ singularity for 2D Boussinesq. Note carefully: this 2D blow-up is for **Boussinesq, not 2D Navier-Stokes**, and it is driven by the buoyancy forcing term, which has no analogue in plain 2D NS. It does not threaten control A (see below).

## Method / structure

The proof is a self-similar / modulation argument anchored on an exactly solvable leading-order model. At the lemma level:

1. **Reduction to the swirl-free vorticity transport.** For axisymmetric no-swirl flow, write the dynamics in terms of $g = \omega^\theta / r$ (the vorticity-over-radius), which is transported by the meridional flow, and a stream function recovered from $g$ by an axisymmetric Biot-Savart law. The full 3D Euler system collapses to a 2D (in the $(r,z)$ meridional half-plane) active-scalar transport problem with a nonlocal velocity. This is the structural reason the construction is tractable: it is "2D-looking" but carries the genuine 3D stretching through the $1/r$ weight.

2. **The fundamental / leading-order model.** Near the origin Elgindi extracts a **leading-order operator**: in the $C^{1,\alpha}$ scaling the singular Biot-Savart kernel, restricted to the leading angular mode, reduces to an explicit **one-dimensional** integral operator. The resulting model (the "fundamental model") is a 1D transport equation whose velocity is given by an explicit integral of the unknown, of the schematic form
$$\partial_t \Omega + c_\alpha\, \big(L[\Omega]\big)\, R\,\partial_R \Omega = c_\alpha\, \big(L[\Omega]\big)\, \Omega,$$
where $R$ is a rescaled radial variable, $L$ is an explicit averaging (log-type) integral operator, and the right-hand side is the **vortex-stretching** term. The point is that $L$ is exactly computable, so the model admits an **explicit self-similar profile**.

3. **The stable self-similar profile.** The fundamental model has a self-similar solution $\Omega(R, t) = \Phi(R) / (T^* - t)$ (the stretching rate is constant in self-similar time, giving a $1/(T^*-t)$ amplitude blow-up) with $\Phi$ an explicit, smooth-away-from-origin, $C^{1,\alpha}$-compatible profile. The amplification coefficient $c_\alpha \sim 1/\alpha$ in the relevant normalization is what makes the stretching win: smaller $\alpha$ gives a stronger leading-order feedback. The profile is shown **linearly and then nonlinearly stable** for the model, which is the heart of the matter.

4. **Closing the loop on the true Euler equation.** The actual 3D Euler dynamics is treated as a perturbation of the fundamental model. Elgindi sets up rescaled (self-similar) coordinates, decomposes the solution as profile-plus-remainder, and controls the remainder in a weighted $C^{1,\alpha}$-type norm via energy estimates for the linearized operator around $\Phi$. The linearized operator has a favorable spectral structure (a controllable unstable/neutral part handled by modulating the blow-up parameters $T^*$ and a scaling rate), and the nonlinear remainder is shown to stay small relative to the profile up to $T^*$. The viscous Laplacian is **absent** throughout; nothing damps the small scales, which is exactly why the collapse closes.

5. **The role of $\omega\cdot\nabla u$.** The right-hand stretching term in the fundamental model is the no-swirl reduction of the full 3D stretching term $(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$. It is genuinely 3D: in 2D incompressible Euler the vorticity is a scalar transported with **no** stretching term, and this construction has no 2D-Euler analogue. The buoyancy forcing in the Boussinesq companion is the device that **manufactures** an effective stretching in a 2D model, which is why Boussinesq, and not 2D Euler/NS, is the planar mirror.

## Criticality placement

Euler has the same scaling symmetry as Navier-Stokes on the velocity, $u_\lambda(x, t) = \lambda\, u(\lambda x, \lambda t)$ for Euler (no $\lambda^2$ in time, since there is no diffusion to fix the parabolic scaling). The project's bookkeeper is built for the **parabolic** NS scaling $u_\lambda(x,t) = \lambda\, u(\lambda x, \lambda^2 t)$; for Euler one uses the hyperbolic scaling, and the relevant statement is about the regularity exponent of the data, not a parabolic Lebesgue norm. The honest placements:

- **The data is at the regularity borderline, not a Lebesgue critical space.** $C^{1,\alpha}$ velocity means $\nabla u \in C^{0,\alpha}$, i.e. $u \in C^{1,\alpha}$, which embeds (locally) into the Holder/Besov scale just **below** the Lipschitz-velocity class $C^{0,1}$ in which 3D Euler is classically locally well-posed (Beale-Kato-Majda, Kato). The construction sits in the thin layer $C^{1,\alpha}$ with $\alpha$ small: enough regularity for local existence and uniqueness (so the solution and its singularity are well-defined), but **below** the smoothness ($C^{1,1}$ / Lipschitz gradient) that the classical no-swirl axisymmetric protection requires. In the project's vocabulary this is an "at-the-margin" object on the **regularity** axis rather than the Lebesgue-criticality axis.

- **Why this matters for the NS supercriticality gap (control B).** The NS energy $\|u\|_{L^2}$ is the only coercive all-time a priori bound and is supercritical: under the NS parabolic scaling `lebesgue_exponent(2, 3)` $= 1 - 3/2 = -1/2 < 0$, so the bookkeeper returns `INSUFFICIENT_BY_ITSELF`. Elgindi's solution is finite-energy, so the **energy is conserved** for the smooth Euler flow up to $T^*$ and is completely compatible with the blow-up: the singularity hides in the higher norms ($\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$, $\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}$) that the energy does not see. This is the same lesson as Tao 2016, made physical: a globally bounded supercritical quantity (energy) coexists with finite-time blow-up, so any control above the energy must be added by hand. Here the missing control on the NS side is precisely dissipation, and the question is whether $\nu\Delta u$ can beat the $1/\alpha$-strength stretching feedback of this profile.

- **The self-similar rate.** The amplitude blows up like $\|\omega\|_{L^\infty} \sim (T^*-t)^{-1}$, the natural self-similar (Type I in amplitude) rate for the stretching equation $D_t\omega = \omega\,\partial$ when the stretching rate is order one in self-similar time. This is the borderline rate at which a hypothetical NS analogue would be Type I, exactly the class probed by the quantitative-regularity / concentration program (Tao 2019; Barker-Prange). The viscous term scales subcritically against this profile in the $C^{1,\alpha}$ class, which is the precise sense in which "viscosity might save NS even though Euler dies here" is an open, quantitative question rather than a theorem.

## Against the three controls

- **(A) 2D control.** Passes cleanly, and in the most informative way. The construction is genuinely 3D: it is driven by the no-swirl reduction of the vortex-stretching term $(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$, carried by the $1/r$ weight in $g = \omega^\theta/r$. Plain 2D incompressible Euler / Navier-Stokes has **no** stretching term ($\omega$ is a transported scalar with non-increasing enstrophy), and this mechanism has no 2D-fluid analogue. The 2D blow-up in the companion is for **Boussinesq**, where the buoyancy forcing $\partial_{x_1}\rho$ synthesizes an effective stretching that 2D NS lacks. So the method does **not** falsely predict 2D Navier-Stokes blow-up. It respects control A exactly: the singularity is built from the structure that distinguishes 3D from 2D.

- **(B) Supercriticality.** Not applicable as a regularity claim (this is a blow-up result, not an a priori bound), but it sharpens control B's meaning. The energy is conserved and compatible with the singularity; the blow-up is invisible to the supercritical energy norm and visible only in the higher norms. This is the Euler-side incarnation of "energy plus scaling cannot decide regularity." It does not supply, and does not pretend to supply, any critical coercive bound.

- **(C) Viscosity / Burgers.** This is the control the paper **is**, in its sharpest 3D form. Inviscid Burgers shocks in finite time; inviscid 3D Euler, by Elgindi, forms a $C^{1,\alpha}$ self-similar singularity by vortex stretching. The viscous Laplacian is absent from the entire construction, and nothing damps the small-scale cascade that drives the collapse. The result therefore isolates the exact scenario that Navier-Stokes dissipation must defeat: a finite-energy, axisymmetric, stretching-driven self-similar collapse at the bottom of the local-well-posedness regularity scale. A candidate NS regularity method that is blind to viscosity, or that would "apply" equally to Euler, is refuted by this theorem, because it would have to rule out a singularity that provably exists for Euler. In control-C terms: Elgindi 2021 is the high-water mark of "what the inviscid world does," and it draws the line that the dissipative estimate must cross.

## What it gives / what it does not give

**What it gives.**

- The first **rigorous** (not numerical) finite-time singularity for finite-energy 3D incompressible Euler, settling that the smooth-flow blow-up question for Euler is genuinely a regularity-class question: blow-up occurs at $C^{1,\alpha}$ and the open frontier is whether it survives up to $C^\infty$ smooth data.
- A **stable, self-similar** mechanism (companion paper), so the singularity is robust, not a fine-tuned coincidence, and it persists under small swirl.
- A precise, explicit **target profile** for the analogous Navier-Stokes question: it tells the NS side exactly which collapse to test dissipation against (axisymmetric, no/small swirl, $\omega^\theta/r$-driven, $1/(T^*-t)$ rate, $C^{1,\alpha}$ class).
- A clean separation of the two planar mirrors: **2D Boussinesq blows up** (forced effective stretching), **2D Euler/NS does not** (no stretching). This is a coordinate that keeps control A honest.

**What it does not give (the gap to closing, or breaking, NS regularity).**

- **It is Euler, not Navier-Stokes.** There is no dissipation anywhere in the argument. It does not prove NS blow-up and does not (by itself) suggest NS blows up. The $1/\alpha$-strength stretching is exactly the term a viscous Laplacian might dominate at small scales; whether it does is the open quantitative question.
- **It needs $\alpha > 0$ and $\alpha$ small.** The result is for low-regularity data; it does **not** establish blow-up for $C^\infty$ smooth Euler data, which remains open (the Hou-Luo / Chen-Hou program addresses the smooth, boundary-driven case; see Lineage). The honest statement is "Euler blows up at $C^{1,\alpha}$," not "Euler blows up for smooth data on $\mathbb{R}^3$."
- **No NS a priori bound and no critical control.** It is a barrier/target on the inviscid side. It does not touch the supercriticality gap (control B) and supplies no coercive critical norm for NS. It tells the BUILDER what to beat, not how to beat it.

The one-line summary for the spine: with viscosity off, 3D incompressible flow provably blows up by vortex stretching at the bottom of the regularity scale, so any Navier-Stokes regularity proof must show dissipation defeats exactly this self-similar collapse. Removing the inviscid escape hatch (the hope that "maybe even Euler is fine") is the coordinate.

## Lineage and sharpest known form

**Builds on.**

- The vortex-stretching transport structure of axisymmetric no-swirl Euler ($\omega^\theta/r$ transported), classical, and the BKM continuation criterion (J. T. Beale, T. Kato, A. Majda, Comm. Math. Phys. 94 (1984), 61-66), which is the regularity threshold the construction crosses. See [`beale_kato_majda_1984.md`](beale_kato_majda_1984.md).
- The 1D "model" tradition for fluid singularities: the Constantin-Lax-Majda model (1985) and the De Gregorio and Cordoba-Cordoba-Fontelos models, which showed that explicit nonlocal 1D transport models can capture stretching-driven blow-up. Elgindi's fundamental model is the rigorous axisymmetric-Euler-derived member of this family.
- The **Hou-Luo numerical scenario**: G. Luo, T. Y. Hou, "Potentially singular solutions of the 3D axisymmetric Euler equations," Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 111 (2014), 12968-12973, which gave high-resolution numerical evidence of a **boundary-driven** axisymmetric Euler singularity (with swirl, on a solid wall, smooth data). Elgindi's theorem is the rigorous cousin in a different regime: **no boundary, no swirl, low regularity**. It does not prove the Hou-Luo scenario; it establishes a provable singularity by a related but distinct mechanism.

**Built on it / refinements through 2025.**

- **Elgindi-Ghoul-Masmoudi**, "On Stability and Instability of $C^{1,\alpha}$ Singular Solutions to the 3D Euler and 2D Boussinesq Equations," Comm. Math. Phys. 405 (2024) (arXiv:1910.14071): stability of the self-similar blow-up (including under small swirl), the 2D Boussinesq analogue, and the companion globally regular Boussinesq solutions. (verify: CMP volume/article.)
- **Chen-Hou (computer-assisted, smooth data)**: J. Chen, T. Y. Hou, "Stable nearly self-similar blowup of the 2D Boussinesq and 3D Euler equations with smooth data I: Analysis," arXiv:2210.07191 (2022); "... II: Rigorous Numerics," Multiscale Model. Simul. (SIAM) (2025), arXiv:2305.05660. This is the rigorous resolution of the **smooth, boundary-driven** (Hou-Luo) regime that Elgindi's $C^{1,\alpha}$ result does not cover: a computer-assisted proof of stable nearly-self-similar finite-time blow-up for 2D Boussinesq and 3D axisymmetric Euler with **smooth** initial data and a boundary. Together, Elgindi (low regularity, no boundary, rigorous-by-hand) and Chen-Hou (smooth data, boundary, computer-assisted) bracket the modern Euler-blow-up picture. See the atlas entry in [`04_blowup_and_barriers.md`](../research_directions/04_blowup_and_barriers.md).
- **ML-discovered profiles**: Y. Wang, C.-Y. Lai, J. Gomez-Serrano, T. Buckmaster, "Asymptotic self-similar blow-up profile for three-dimensional axisymmetric Euler equations using neural networks," Phys. Rev. Lett. 130 (2023), 244002: physics-informed neural networks find smooth self-similar profiles (including unstable ones) for 2D Boussinesq and 3D Euler, complementing the rigorous constructions.
- **Toward Navier-Stokes**: a 2026 preprint program (arXiv:2604.09949, "Stable Finite-Time Singularity Formation for 3D Navier-Stokes") claims to push a related stable self-similar mechanism into the dissipative setting; if it holds up it would be a control-C-crossing event for NS itself. (verify: status, scope, whether it concerns the standard equations or a modified/forced system; treat as unconfirmed pending VERIFIER review. Do not cite as settled.)

**Sharpest known form (2025).** Rigorous finite-time singularity for 3D incompressible Euler is established for finite-energy $C^{1,\alpha}$ axisymmetric no-swirl data (Elgindi 2021), stably and including the 2D Boussinesq analogue (Elgindi-Ghoul-Masmoudi 2024), and for **smooth** data with a boundary via computer-assisted proof (Chen-Hou, 2022/2025). Blow-up for $C^\infty$ smooth Euler data on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with no boundary remains open. Finite-time singularity for the **Navier-Stokes** equations remains open (no rigorous result), and that gap is exactly the viscosity question this dossier isolates.
